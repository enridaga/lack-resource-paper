\vspace{-0.3cm}\section{Evaluation}
\label{sec:evaluation}
We evaluate LACK along two dimensions: coverage with respect to the competency questions, and utility, through a questionnaire with domain experts.
\subsection{Resource Coverage}
We assess the extent to which the KG answers the competency questions that drove its design.
For each CQ, Table~\ref{tab:coverage} reports the ontology relation(s) involved, triple counts before and after inferencing, the number of distinct entities affected, and the resulting coverage ratio (entities affected over the 27{,}974 total entities; for the temporal CQ10, reified statements carrying \texttt{lack:since}/\texttt{lack:until} over 51{,}848 total reified statements). Counts are obtained via SPARQL queries against the ClimateSense endpoint\footnote{\url{https://sparql.climatesense.kmi.tools/climatesense}, graph \texttt{<https://purl.net/climatesense/lack>}}.
We classify CQs into three tiers --- \textbf{Strong}, \textbf{Moderate}, \textbf{Weak} --- using natural breaks in the empirical distribution of coverage ratios: the largest gap (7.5\,pp, between 12.2\% and 4.7\%) separates Weak from the rest, and the next largest (7.2\,pp, between 22.8\% and 15.6\%) separates Strong from Moderate.
CQ9 is excluded from this partition and reported separately, since \texttt{associatedWith} is the top-level relation in the hierarchy and its coverage is heavily inflated by inference; CQ1 is degenerate (every entity satisfies it) and reported as N/A.

\paragraph{Discussion}
%Coverage is strong across transparency and network-structure CQs.
Coverage is solid across the core transparency and network-structure CQs, with employment, membership, leadership, and temporal annotations all in the Strong tier.
The most striking result is network reach (CQ9): after inferencing, 93.5\% of entities are reachable via \texttt{associatedWith}, transforming a sparse set of assertions into a near-complete connectivity structure; employment (CQ5) similarly benefits via the \texttt{leadsAt} sub-property hierarchy.
Temporal coverage is partial: 14{,}361 reified statements carry \texttt{lack:since}, enabling meaningful time-bounded queries, while entity-level bounds (\texttt{activeSince}/\texttt{activeUntil}) cover only 2.6\% of entities and should be treated as supplementary.
Founding relations (CQ7) remain sparse, reflecting the difficulty of reliably extracting founding events.
The composite CQ10+CQ5 row is listed separately because tracing movement between organisations requires both employment and temporal reification jointly.

\vspace{-0.2cm}\subsection{Domain experts' feedback}
A 14-question online questionnaire was distributed via purposive sampling to researchers, fact-checkers, investigative journalists, NGO professionals, and policy analysts working on climate lobbying networks, across 25 organisational groups spanning academic networks, fact-checking organisations, investigative outlets, NGOs, and international bodies.
Of 68 invitations, 9 were completed (13.2\%); while small, the sample spans the primary user communities, and the consistency of responses across professional backgrounds lends confidence to the findings.

% \paragraph{Demand validation.} Respondents unanimously confirmed the value of a structured, searchable resource for climate lobbying networks, rating its overall importance at M=4.62 on a 5-point scale. The ability to search across multiple sources in a single system was rated equally highly (M=4.62), directly validating the integration approach that underpins LACK.

\vspace{-0.3cm}\paragraph{Demand validation.} Respondents unanimously rated both the overall value of such a resource and the ability to search across multiple sources at M=4.62 on a 5-point scale, directly validating LACK's integration approach.

% \paragraph{Priority relation types.} When asked to rate the importance of different connection types, funding flows received the highest mean rating (M=4.75), followed by employment history (M=4.50), membership (M=4.38), sponsorship (M=4.38), and founding relations (M=4.38). Comparing these priorities with the coverage statistics in Table~\ref{tab:coverage} reveals both strengths and gaps. Funding relations (CQ2), the top-rated category, are represented by 5,210 asserted triples covering 3,421 entities, classified as \textbf{Strong}. Employment (CQ5) and membership (CQ3) similarly show \textbf{Strong} coverage with 10,943 and 9,718 affected entities respectively, well-matched to expert demand. However, founding relations (CQ7), rated 4.38 by experts, represent the most significant gap: only 73 asserted triples covering 108 entities (\textbf{Weak}), reflecting the difficulty of reliably extracting founding events from unstructured source text. This mismatch between high user demand and low extraction yield identifies founding data as a priority for future enrichment, potentially through targeted manual curation or dedicated information extraction pipelines.

% \paragraph{Temporal and geographic dimensions.} Temporal information (when connections started and ended) received a mean importance rating of 3.88 and was flagged by half the respondents as one of the hardest types of information to find. Statement-level temporal annotation (CQ10) reaches the Strong tier (27.7\%), but entity-level activity bounds (CQ11) cover only 2.6\% of entities --- consistent with respondents' acknowledgement that reliable temporal evidence is scarce even in the source material, despite being critical for tracking revolving doors and organisational evolution.

\vspace{-0.2cm}\paragraph{Use cases and the political sphere.} The most frequently selected CQs concerned funding chains (5/9), revolving doors between industry and government (4/9), and multi-hop connections between corporations and advocacy groups (4/9), the latter aligning well with LACK's demonstrated network reach (CQ9, 93.5\%).

Respondents also recommended distinguishing intermediary actor types (lobby consultancies, law firms, PR agencies) currently not separated in the ontology.

Overall, the feedback validates LACK's core design --- multi-source integration and the emphasis on employment and membership (\textbf{Strong}) alongside funding (\textbf{Moderate}) --- while highlighting three priorities, all reflected in the conclusion's future-work agenda: deepening funding coverage, extending entity-level temporal annotation, and connecting to the political sphere.
