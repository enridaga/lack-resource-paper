The construction process surfaces challenges characteristic of this domain: lobbying networks are dynamic, and the two dimensions our evaluation and our experts both found hardest, temporal information and funding ties, are the most difficult to find in the sources.
%Lobbying networks are dynamic, and the two dimensions our evaluation and our experts both found hardest, temporal information and funding ties, are precisely those that the sources disclose obliquely or not at all.
The long tail of minor actors, obscure consultants, short-lived coalitions, and regional trade bodies drives the high proportion of entities without external identifiers. 
This is an open research problem: enriching and disambiguating long-tail entities absent from general-purpose knowledge bases is an active challenge for automatic knowledge graph construction~\cite{graciotti2025lost}.
The reliance on English-language sources introduces a geographic and linguistic skew, visible in our COP30 cross-reference, where actors from lower-income countries are markedly under-represented.

Our evaluation establishes a preliminary quality baseline rather than a complete picture, and the next release will extend it in four directions: breaking accuracy down by relation type, evaluating LobbyMap extraction separately from DeSmog, measuring end-to-end recall against manually annotated pages, and auditing the COP30 label matching for precision and recall.
A comparison of the entity-linking pipeline against dedicated baselines and a broader expert survey is planned.

LACK repurposes information produced by organisations that invest considerable effort in it, and respecting that effort is of primary importance: we followed DeSmog's republishing guidelines and complied with the redistribution terms we obtained from InfluenceMap.
Thus, we publish LACK under a Creative Commons Attribution-NonCommercial licence.

Where commercial actors routinely ingest others' content without credit, a BY-NC licence preserves attribution and reserves commercial rights to the original owners, which we hope makes it easier for further investigative organisations to contribute to a shared LACK data space.
Because LACK asserts relations about named individuals and organisations, extraction errors carry reputational risk; surfacing per-statement confidence and evidence in the interface, and providing a route for subjects and source organisations to flag errors, are planned before wider promotion.
The gaps our experts identified, direct links to the political sphere, finer distinctions between intermediary actor types such as consultancies and PR agencies, and integration of further sources such as the EU Transparency Register and OpenSecrets, define our agenda for that growth.
