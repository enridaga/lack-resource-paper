\begin{abstract}
Climate accountability work, carried out by investigative journalists, NGOs, and watchdog organisations, depends on understanding who lobbies against climate action and how these actors are connected. 
While the evidence exists, documented in depth by platforms such as DeSmog and LobbyMap, it remains fragmented across sources, heterogeneous in form, and difficult to analyse at scale. 
We present LACK (Lobbying Agents, Climate, and Knowledge), a knowledge graph of $\sim$28k entities and $\sim$200k typed relations (covering affiliations, funding ties, managers, and partnerships), built from over 2k web pages and, to our knowledge, the first knowledge graph dedicated to climate lobbying networks. 
By making these relationships queryable, LACK supports the tasks practitioners actually perform: tracing funding flows, tracking how actors' positions shift over time, and mapping influence networks across organisations, turning fragmented records into actionable intelligence. 
We demonstrate LACK through a set of case studies and validate its utility with domain experts from the investigative journalism and climate accountability sector.
Beyond the resource itself, we discuss the opportunities and challenges of building large knowledge bases automatically in this domain, drawing on our engagement with practitioners in the sector.
